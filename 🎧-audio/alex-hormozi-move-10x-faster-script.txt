How to Move 10x Faster by Alex Hormozi.

What is the "three generations" reminder?

In three generations, everyone who knew us will be dead, including the people whose opinions stopped us from doing what we wanted. Whether we accomplish our goals or not, the result is the same — we'll be forgotten.


What does thinking about death actually do?

It influences how we see time and what actions we're willing to take despite the judgment of others. In three generations we'll be forgotten, and the people who nayed against us will also be dead. So do it for yourself — there's only one voice to listen to.


Why is thinking from first principles like moving your ears?

All humans have the muscle for independent thinking, but because no one trains it as a child, it atrophies. The more you practice thinking from first principles, the more it becomes your default way of thinking — and everyone else's choices start looking increasingly insane.


What does Alex find terrifying about an "acceptable" life?

Walking through life on autopilot sounds more terrifying to him than failing over and over again. The freedom to fail repeatedly feels more fulfilling than living a life that is "okay" but isn't yours.


What is the Rule of 7?

Don't follow what most people do, because you don't want the results that most people get. The average person is obese, likely to be divorced, and has less than $1,000 in the bank. It feels safe to do what everyone else is doing, but it's actually a terrible decision.


Why is the first wall the hardest?

The bigger the dragon you slay, the more evidence you have that you can slay the next one. After Alex quit his job and left his father, selling to strangers seemed easy. After that, facing partners seemed easy. Each wall overcome fuels the next.


What happened when Alex quit his job?

He didn't tell his father until he was five states away. He was so afraid of his father's disapproval that he physically left the state before having the conversation. The time between deciding to change and actually changing just keeps compressing.


What is the pattern Alex keeps repeating?

Every painful experience in his life has created a disproportionate gain. Quitting his job led to his first business. The DUI led him out of failed partnerships. If things can't get worse, your activation energy drops and you finally do what you should have done.


What is Region Beta Paradox?

If you travel a mile or less, you walk. If more than a mile, you drive. Paradoxically, you cover two miles faster than one mile. People only act when things cross a threshold of badness. Sometimes better things feel worse than worse things.


Why would people be better off if their situations were worse?

Because it would give them the activation energy to leave. The person in the "okay" job, the "acceptable" relationship, the apartment with mold — their only regret would be not doing it sooner. Comfort is the enemy of action.


How did a near-death experience change Alex?

After the DUI head-on collision, he realized the stress from avoiding conversations almost killed him. The next day, he called his first partner to end the partnership. The courage came from: "I'd rather be alive." Death gave him the urgency to act.


What is the "zoom out" perspective?

If you zoom out far enough from Earth, you can't see it anymore. All the things you're worried about become invisible at that scale. It immediately puts into perspective how ridiculous some of your concerns are.


What does Alex do when he feels anxious or insecure?

He asks himself: "What conversation do I need to have that I'm not having?" Usually he knows immediately which conversation he's been putting off. Then he has it. The time between knowing and acting keeps compressing.


What creates freedom to act despite fear?

Reframing "I have nothing going for me" as "I have nothing to lose." If you feel like you're at the bottom, that makes you a more dangerous person — because you have no reason not to take action. That flip has repeated throughout Alex's life.


What is first-principles thinking and why does it atrophy?

You can no longer rely on societal norms, role models, archetypes, or expectations. You have to think for yourself. Like a muscle you never trained, it's there but atrophied. The more you practice it, the more your default thinking changes.


Why do people live on autopilot?

It feels safe to do what everyone else is doing. But the average person's life — divorced, obese, broke — isn't the life anyone actually wants. The best way to guarantee not having the life you want is to do what everyone else does.


What separates heroes from villains?

Same background: pain. The hero says "I won't let anyone else feel what I felt." The villain says "I'll make everyone feel what I felt." Heroes use pain. Villains are used by it.


How did Alex use the success as revenge?

He was bullied brutally in school. He developed a chip on his shoulder and wanted to prove to the world he was worth something. That drove him for a decade. Is it toxic long-term? Yes. Did it work? Also yes. Use the energy to start, then find a better motor.


What are the three traits of highly successful people?

Superiority complex ("I can achieve this"), massive inadequacy ("I'm not enough"), and impulse control. The inadequacy is the cat that chases you — it's more powerful than motivation. Use it.


What is "wringing the knife"?

The things that bother us have an element of truth we don't want to look at. Wringing the knife is looking in the mirror and asking: "What if they're right?" That's where growth happens.


What conversation do most people avoid?

The few hard conversations that stand between them and the life they want. You're living a life you hate because you're too afraid to have them. Alex uses this as a self-diagnostic: when he feels bad, he asks what conversation he's avoiding.


What happened after Alex stood up to his father?

Having crossed the biggest wall (defying his father's expectations by quitting his job), every subsequent hard conversation felt easier. The evidence from the first dragon gave him confidence for the next one.


What does the DUI experience reveal about stress?

"All of their livelihoods were still dependent on me but I was splitting everything and it was horrible." The stress from avoiding the conversation of ending partnerships nearly killed him. His wakeup call wasn't to stop drinking — it was to have the conversations he'd been avoiding.


How does Alex approach fear of judgment from others?

In three generations, anyone who judged you will be dead. If they'll be dead soon, their judgment is temporary. But the life you build is yours. Do it for yourself — there's only one voice to listen to.


What is the relationship between pain and growth?

Pain is the input. Every painful experience in Alex's life has created a disproportionate gain. Don't waste it. If you're going through the worst moment of your life right now, this is the moment that forges what you're going to become.


How do you reframe hitting bottom?

"I have nothing going for me" also means "I have nothing to lose." That makes you a more dangerous, more decisive person. People at the bottom who reframe it this way take massive action because there's no downside left.


Why do imitators always stay behind?

They copy what works without understanding why it worked. When it breaks, they can't fix it because they didn't build it. Copy the cause, not the result. Think from first principles and build something yours.


How does the "woman in the red dress" apply to focus?

Like in The Matrix — Neo walks through a black-and-white city and suddenly a woman in a red dress walks by. He can't help but look. She turns out to be Agent Smith with a gun to his head. Distractions become more attractive the more successful you get.


What should be your filter for decisions?

"If it's not a billion, it's not worth my time." Alex gets 3,000 companies a month wanting deals. He's done 13 in two years. The filter: if it's not worth a billion, don't touch it. Scale this to everything in your life.


What are the most powerful questions to ask yourself?

"What would I do if I weren't afraid?" or "What conversation am I not having that I need to have?" These two questions unlock most of the paralysis people experience.


What is the key to hard conversations?

The day you have the dreaded conversation, the moment after, you think "that wasn't as bad as I thought — what else can I do?" Each hard conversation makes the next one easier. The dread is always worse than the reality.


How does agency compound?

The more you practice taking agency and responsibility for decisions, the better you get at it. What seemed impossible a decade ago — quitting a job, leaving a relationship — now feels immediate. The timeline between decision and action compresses.


What is alchemy in this context?

Turning something worse than useless into something as valuable as possible. The DUI became clarity. Leaving his father became a business. Losing everything led to the right model. Your worst moment is raw material.


How does society reward the status quo?

With promotions, approval, and social acceptance. Alex's father was most proud when Alex was most miserable (consulting job, doing everything "right"). If the system's approval produces misery, it's not the right compass.


What happens when you practice saying no?

It's not really a "no" muscle — it's a "yes" muscle for the one thing that matters. Once you define your one yes, everything else becomes a no. Alex says no to 2,987 out of 3,000 opportunities a month.


Why do some things that are good feel worse than things that are bad?

Because of Region Beta — you only act when pain crosses a threshold. A mediocre job feels comfortable enough not to leave. A terrible job would force action. The better-feeling option keeps you stuck.


What is the cost of not having the conversation?

"It could literally kill you." Alex's stress from avoiding hard conversations with his partners nearly killed him in a car accident. The avoidance was more dangerous than the confrontation.


What does Alex mean by "disproportionate gain"?

The worst moments of his life produced the biggest growth. Quitting his job → first business. The DUI → ended bad partnerships. Losing everything → found the right model. Pain, properly used, compounds.


What was the moment Alex decided to change?

When his father was most proud of him — and he was the saddest. That was the signal. If the path someone else approves of produces sadness, it's not your path. His approval isn't your compass.


How do you know when it's time to leave?

"If living an okay life sounds terrifying, you already know." If your body and mind are telling you something is wrong, they're probably right. The conversation you're avoiding is the one you need to have most.


What is the most common form of revenge?

"Success is the only revenge." Not confrontation. Build something so big that the people who doubted you shrink into irrelevance. Don't defeat them — project a shadow so large they disappear.


How did Alex's first hard conversation lead to others?

After calling his first partner to end the partnership, he immediately called the second. And the third. The energy from the first courageous action fueled the rest. One brave conversation creates momentum for all the others.


What should you do when you have nothing to lose?

Take massive action. Having nothing to lose is actually the most powerful position — because there's no downside. Most people at the bottom don't realize this is their greatest advantage.


How often does Alex think about the hard conversations he's avoiding?

"When I feel anxious or insecure or angry or sad, I ask myself: what conversation do I need to have that I'm not having?" Emotional discomfort is usually a signal that a conversation is overdue.


What is the real cost of avoiding discomfort?

You live a life you don't want. Region Beta keeps you in mediocrity. The comfortable prison feels safe but it's still a prison. The activation energy to leave never decreases — it only gets harder the longer you wait.


What does "stew on it" mean for Alex?

Sleeping on decisions is overrated. If you want to change something, change it. Done. The time between deciding and acting should be as short as possible. Walk out of the meeting and decide — don't sleep on it.


Why do people avoid big life changes?

Because they're comparing the pain of change against the pain of staying. The pain of staying feels smaller in the moment, but it compounds over decades. The pain of changing is sharp but short. Choose the short, sharp pain.


What does Alex tell younger people about their parents' opinions?

"I imagine how much you care about your mother's and father's opinion. Now imagine there's literally only one. And he disapproves. How do you discount that one voice?" His trick: invert the situation to understand the weight you're giving opinions.


What is Alex's approach to conflict?

"Conflict is just energy. You can use it to build or destroy. People avoid conflict because they think it's bad. But if someone is standing between you and what you want, that conflict is necessary."


Why is "first principles" thinking so rare?

Because societal norms are easier. Following what everyone else does feels safe. But first principles thinking atrophies like an unused muscle. Most people never flex it — not because they can't, but because they never practice.


What happens when you transform your story?

"The storyteller rules the king." The narrative you tell yourself determines what you do. Change the story and you change the behavior. Pain can be the villain or the teacher — the facts don't change, just the story around them.


What is the paradox of good opportunities?

The best opportunities often look like the worst ones. A scary conversation, a risky move, a big change — they feel terrible but lead to the biggest growth. Meanwhile, comfortable options keep you in Region Beta.


What can we learn from Alex's 6 months of change?

Between deciding to change his life and actually changing it, there were 6 months. Change isn't instantaneous — there's a gap between decision and action. But during those 6 months, the change is already happening internally.


How to handle people who try to minimize your ambitions?

"The loudest voices saying you can't are usually the ones who didn't." When people tell you your dreams are too big, they're projecting their own limitations. Those voices have an element of truth you don't want to hear — wring the knife.


What was Alex's experience with failing publicly?

"When I got the DUI and had to tell everyone, I was humiliated. But it also forced me to have conversations I'd been avoiding for two years. The failure that felt like the end was actually the beginning of massive growth."


How do you know which pain to pay attention to?

"The pain that has a story behind it." Not all pain is equal. The pain of a hard conversation that's been avoided for years is the most important kind. The discomfort is a signal pointing to where the growth is.
